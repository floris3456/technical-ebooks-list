# Algorithm Design — first-edition work, deferred

## Decision and audience

**Strong candidate, not counted.** The inspected publisher contents and independent graduate-course use support a problem-driven design text for readers already comfortable with programming, data structures and proof arguments [T1–T3]. The concrete attraction is progression from stable matching and exchange arguments to interval/sequence dynamic programming, network-flow models and hardness reductions, rather than a language-specific code tutorial [T1].

The remaining evidence gap is substantive inspection of an original worked argument/example and its relevant corrections. The publisher TOC and course reading lists establish scope/use, not the correctness or exposition of that material. Repeated legitimate author/publisher preview failures did not produce that missing evidence [T5]. This is **not a poor-quality verdict, an age preference or an availability/price rejection**.

## Scope, relevance and alternatives

Primary subject: Algorithms & Data Structures; graphs and proof are parts of that teaching objective, not incidental extra categories. Current graph-tool and NetworkX contracts still make graph structure, weights and algorithm preconditions operational concerns [T4]. That supports the continuing applicability of the inspected topic structure, not an endorsement of unseen book prose.

This work's problem-driven organization would distinguish it from CLRS's broader reference, DPV's compressed conceptual course, Skiena's problem catalog and Sedgewick–Wayne's executable Java approach. These are comparisons of inspected contents and educational evidence, not claimed cover-to-cover experience. Do not approve it solely to fill another algorithms slot.

## Identity, edition and limitations

The same storefront labels its electronic product9780137546350 as published2021-07-14/copyright2022. Those platform dates are not proof of a revised second edition or of exact equivalence to every2005 printing [T1]. Only the unambiguous paperback identifier is stored by format.

Georgia Tech's Fall2026 syllabus recommends this work while requiring DPV; it does not require KT cover to cover [T2]. NUS's2013 course says it may use parts of the Pearson International Edition, not that it assigned the current US electronic build [T3]. Neither source resolves errata. No relevant errata body was successfully inspected; absence of such a read is not proof of correctness. The bounded search did not establish a replacement edition, and no universal latest-edition guarantee is made.
